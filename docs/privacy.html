Horde Survival Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 9, 2026

This policy explains how the Horde Survival mobile game ("the app") for Android and iOS handles your information. In short: the app does not collect any personal information. The only thing sent off your device is an anonymous high score for the global leaderboard.

Information we collect

The app does not ask for or collect your name, email, location, contacts, photos, advertising or device identifiers, or any other personal information. It has no user accounts.

Global leaderboard

When you beat your best score, overall, for the current week, or in the day's challenge, the app sends the following to our leaderboard server so it can rank you against other players:

  • A random player ID created by the app the first time it runs. It is not linked to you, your device, or any account.
  • Your score, the wave you reached, and how long the run lasted.
  • For a daily challenge run, which day's challenge it was.

Your leaderboard name (for example, "SwiftOtter482") is generated automatically from the random ID. You can change it in the app, but only by picking words and a number from built-in lists: a name cannot be typed, so it cannot contain personal details. If you pick a name, it is sent to the server along with the random ID. After a game the app also asks the server for your current rank, using the random ID. Names, scores, and waves on the leaderboard are visible to everyone who plays. The server runs on Cloudflare, which processes network requests on our behalf and may briefly keep standard request logs under its own privacy policy.

We do not store your IP address. To keep the leaderboard from being flooded with fake entries, the server limits how many new players one network address can add:

  • When a new player ID sends its first score, the server turns the IP address of the request into a short scrambled code, using a random key that changes every day, and counts how many new players that code has added that day. The code is too short to be turned back into an address: thousands of addresses share each code. Only the code and the count are kept, and they are deleted, together with the key, within a few hours of the end of the day (UTC).
  • Cloudflare counts how many requests an address sends within one minute, to block bursts. That count is held in memory and is gone after the minute.

Leaderboard entries are kept while the leaderboard is running. Because the ID is random and anonymous, we cannot tell which entry is yours unless you contact us with your leaderboard name; if you do, we will delete that entry.

Information stored on your device

The app saves your best score, your coins and upgrades, your achievements, your best daily challenge score, its random player ID, and the leaderboard name you picked (if any) on your device. Deleting the app removes them; reinstalling gives you a new anonymous ID.

Analytics, advertising, and tracking

The app contains no advertising, no analytics or crash-reporting services, and no third-party tracking. It does not track you across other apps or websites.

Network use

The app works fully offline. It only goes online to submit a new best score, to show the leaderboard and your rank, and to save a name you picked. A score made offline is sent the next time you're connected.

Third parties

We do not sell or share any data. Leaderboard data is stored with Cloudflare, our hosting provider, only to run the leaderboard.

Children's privacy

The app is suitable for all ages. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children under 13 (or the equivalent age in your country). Leaderboard names are generated automatically or picked from built-in word lists, so players cannot enter personal details there.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app changes how it handles information, this page will be updated and the date above will change.
